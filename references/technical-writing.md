# Technical writing

Use for requested documentation, READMEs, design proposals, and authorized commit or PR descriptions. Follow Suri's existing communication guidance; this reference adds document organization rather than another style checklist.

- Establish the intended reader and what they need to do or understand. Match the depth to that need, including additional context for unfamiliar backend, database, or infrastructure topics.
- For procedures, state required prerequisites and conditions before the relevant action. Use ordered steps when sequence matters and describe the observable result needed to know whether the step worked.
- Organize instructions, lookup information, and explanation so readers can find each. Separate sections or files only when that improves navigation; do not impose a document taxonomy on every short guide.
- Use actual command names, symbols, paths, and configuration keys. Verify examples against available repository evidence and identify unexecuted commands or environment-dependent assumptions. Do not present illustrative placeholders as working instructions.
- Keep terminology consistent and make pronouns, conditions, ownership, and alternatives unambiguous. Preserve necessary qualifications and links to evidence rather than shortening away meaning.
- Follow the repository's established structure and code-example formatting. Avoid arbitrary sentence-length limits, forced formatting changes, or edits to unrelated prose.
- In commit and PR descriptions, explain what changed, the relevant reason or constraint, and verification evidence or limitations. Preserve useful rationale required by the no-comments policy. Writing guidance does not authorize a commit, PR update, or publication.

Before handing off, check that instructions and examples match the described behavior and that relevant links resolve when accessible. Report what could not be verified. Do not add scripts or regenerate documentation solely to satisfy a formatting preference.
