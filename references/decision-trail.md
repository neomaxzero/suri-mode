# Decision trail

Keep a concise trail when a task is long-running, autonomous, or explicitly needs review after the fact. Reuse its existing progress or human-gate record; do not create competing logs or require a particular format.

- Record meaningful decisions, changes of direction, blockers, and completed verification checkpoints. Skip routine tool calls, self-evident edits, and repeated unchanged status.
- Capture what was chosen, why, the evidence location, and the observed result or unresolved status. Keep entries short without omitting information essential to judge the decision.
- Distinguish planned actions from completed work. Do not invent timestamps, elapsed effort, outcomes, or evidence to fill a template.
- Update the record at meaningful points so another session can recover it. Correct mistaken claims transparently, preserving the relevant correction or superseding decision rather than presenting a false history.
- Before handoff, check material claims against available artifacts and task history. Follow the evidence references that support completion or important decisions. Do not claim access to an unavailable transcript or search unrelated private conversations.
- Keep the trail local by default. Do not commit or publish it without authorization. Follow the existing task location and explicit staging conventions so a requested code commit does not accidentally include the trail.
- No separate reviewer or model is required solely because a trail exists. Apply Suri's proportional review and model policy if uncertainty or impact warrants an independent check.

This record explains the current task. Repository `lessons.md` stores useful confirmed corrections across tasks; do not copy every decision into it. In the final response, mention only decisions, remaining risks, or evidence that matter to the handoff, and link the record when useful.
